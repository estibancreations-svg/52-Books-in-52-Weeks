# True Stories — Series Bible

**Created:** 2026-10-05  
**Project:** 52 Books in 52 Weeks  
**Status:** Active creative framework

## Purpose

*True Stories* preserves the conversations, jokes, warnings, sayings, contradictions, faith, failures, observations, and hard-earned lessons that move from one generation to another.

The series is not nostalgia for nostalgia's sake. Its job is to show **how wisdom is actually transferred** — often imperfectly, sometimes humorously, sometimes painfully, and often before the younger person fully understands what they have been given.

## Primary Voices

Potential source voices include:
- grandparents and great-grandparents,
- parents and older relatives,
- family friends,
- elders,
- teachers,
- mentors,
- neighbors,
- community members,
- strangers whose words became unforgettable,
- and the narrator's younger self.

## Tone

The series can move between:
- funny,
- witty,
- intimate,
- observational,
- uncomfortable,
- tender,
- spiritual,
- painful,
- reflective,
- and instructive.

The dominant mode should be **story first, wisdom earned second**.

## The True Stories Chapter Engine

Every chapter should answer most of these:

### 1. What happened?
A concrete scene, not a generalized memory.

### 2. What was said?
Preserve distinctive phrasing where remembered.

### 3. What did I notice?
The detail that made the moment stick.

### 4. What did younger me think it meant?
Do not give the child/adolescent narrator adult hindsight too early.

### 5. What question did it create?
Curiosity is a recurring engine of the series.

### 6. What did life later teach me?
The delayed interpretation.

### 7. What principle survived?
The distilled lesson.

### 8. What do I do with it now?
Application, stewardship, inheritance, or changed behavior.

## Hook Standard

A strong opening should create a gap between what the reader sees and what the reader knows.

Example pattern:

> Everyone laughed. I did too. But I noticed something — and once I noticed it, I couldn't stop asking why.

The next page earns the reveal.

## Truth & Reconstruction Standard

Before publication, each story receives a source label:

- **Remembered Directly** — narrator's personal memory.
- **Recorded / Verifiable** — supported by text, photo, recording, document, or another source.
- **Family Retelling** — received through oral history.
- **Reconstructed Dialogue** — exact words are not fully remembered; dialogue is rebuilt to preserve meaning and context.
- **Composite / Semi-Fiction** — people, timing, or scenes are intentionally combined or dramatized.
- **Reflection** — present-day interpretation rather than historical fact.

The manuscript should never quietly present invention as verbatim memory.

## Recurring Themes

- “When people show you who they are, believe them.”
- Curiosity as a life engine.
- Identity and recognition.
- Humor as a teaching tool.
- The difference between hearing advice and understanding it.
- Faith and prayer.
- Purpose.
- Discernment.
- Family mythology versus lived truth.
- What age changes about memory.
- What elders were trying to say.
- The lessons we resist.
- The lessons we become.
- Stewardship.
- Passing knowledge forward.

## The “Why” Thread

The question “why” works at three levels:

1. **Human why** — why do people behave, believe, hide, reveal, love, judge, forgive, repeat, or change?
2. **Personal why** — why am I drawn to this possibility, story, project, person, or path?
3. **Purpose why** — what is this work supposed to do beyond benefiting me?

For Book 01, the deepest answer should lead toward **purpose, prayer, service, and stewardship** without forcing a predetermined conclusion onto every story.

## Writing Rule

Do not write the moral first and manufacture a story to fit it.

Start with the human moment. Let the meaning emerge from what actually happened.
